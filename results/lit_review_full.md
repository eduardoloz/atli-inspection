# Literature Review: Deep Learning for Transmission-Line Defect Detection

**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Scope:** 18 local PDFs read in full (the APET2025 ATLI paper, the PI's `ATLI/` paper folder, and the Vazquez UNLV thesis) plus 30 cited works researched via web search.
**Method:** Agent fan-out — one subagent per full-text PDF and per citation-research batch; results collected and synthesized here by the orchestrator. All numeric claims are taken verbatim from the source papers; citations use the BibTeX keys in `paper/refs.bib`.
**Project context:** This review supports the extension of the APET2025 ATLI paper [tsai2025atli]. This summer's work targeted the rare `Defective_Damper` class (113 instances vs 1,460 `Normal_Damper`); external/community data never helped (domain/label mismatch), and the winning recipe was native-only augmentation — imgsz 1280 (later 768 for edge), 3× oversampling of damper images, scale=0.9 aug — giving proper-5-fold-CV mAP@0.5 0.785 ± 0.023 and Defective_Damper AP 0.812 ± 0.080 on YOLOv11n (2.6M params), tying DINO-4scale (47M) on mAP and beating it on the rare class. OBB variants, CPLID decontamination of the test set, and TensorRT FP16 edge deployment (~18 fps projected on Jetson) were also studied.

## 2. Datasets and the data-scarcity problem

Annotated-data scarcity is the founding framing of the field. The canonical reviews [nguyen2018autonomous, liu2020review, yang2020powerline] all identify the lack of large public annotated inspection datasets as the central obstacle to deep vision methods for power lines, and the 2025 survey quantifies it: across 73 reviewed papers, only 23% use public datasets, only 30% exceed 5,000 images, and just 8% release code [faisal2025powerline]. The ATLI dataset (732 images, 7 classes, defect-spot labels) [tsai2025atli] therefore sits squarely in the field's typical regime, not below it.

The public dataset landscape is thin and skewed toward normal components. CPLID [tao2020insulator] is the field's workhorse (1,956 images in the source paper; commonly cited as 848 insulator images), but 996 of its 1,056 defective images are synthetic composites — a fact that matters when interpreting any CPLID-derived benchmark, and directly relevant to this project because ~200 CPLID images were absorbed into ATLI and had to be purged from the test set (the decontamination workstream). CPLID-family benchmarks are effectively saturated: SSD/Faster R-CNN reach 98.17–98.31% mAP on CPLID and 100% on the Tomaszewski set [vieiraesilva2021stnplad], and a 2025 YOLOv9 variant reports 99.66% mAP on CPLID [pradeep2025insulator]. RSIn (1,887 images, 4 insulator types, no defect labels) [shuang2023rsin], TTPLA (towers/lines, instance segmentation) [abdelfattah2020ttpla], and STN PLAD (133 hi-res images, 2,409 instances incl. 1,505 Stockbridge dampers, normal assets only) [vieiraesilva2021stnplad] round out the public options; none ships labeled defective dampers as a paper-published resource.

Damper-defect datasets in the literature are private and tiny: DVDI (976 raw UAV images, 636 damaged-damper instances; public on GitHub) [bao2022bcyolo], DAVD [bao2021pmayolo], TLD (490 images) [zhang2024dsanet], TLCD (184 hammer-damage annotations) [zhang2024hcvit], Wire_10 [liang2020wire10], TLDD (private, 2,306 damper images) [su2024cessd], and Felect (783 "missing knob" instances) [maduako2022felect]. Dataset overlap is an under-acknowledged hazard: RSIn incorporates 848 Git/CPLID-lineage images [shuang2023rsin], ATLI's internet images recycle DVDI (the project's pHash vetting found ~50% of DVDI already inside ATLI), and CPLID feeds multiple derived sets [qiu2022lightweightyolov4, shakiba2022insulator, su2024cessd] — so cross-dataset training or evaluation without a leakage gate risks inflated numbers. No paper in this collection reports a leakage audit of the kind this project performs.

Scarcity workarounds in the literature fall into four families: synthetic defect generation (U-Net segmentation + background fusion in CPLID [tao2020insulator]; Photoshop-simulated faults [maduako2022felect, shakiba2022insulator]; GraphCut copy-paste [qiu2022lightweightyolov4]; simulated missing-cap images [han2020eagle]; WGAN feature synthesis [gao2025zeroshot]), heavy augmentation (~56,000 augmented samples in IN-YOLO [sadykova2020inyolo]; zoom-in cropping for scarce nests [li2020birdnesttower]), transfer learning (Section 4), and self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled normal imagery (~174k crops in HC-ViT [zhang2024hcvit]).

## 3. Detector architectures

**One-stage YOLO family.** YOLO variants dominate the applied literature: the survey's Sankey analysis shows insulator + UAV + 1,000–5,000 images + bounding boxes + YOLO/R-CNN is the field's dominant recipe [faisal2025powerline]. Representative systems: modified YOLOv3 on a drone [siddiqui2020drone], IN-YOLO (YOLOv2) for real-time insulators [sadykova2020inyolo], YOLOv5 with SE attention and a small-target layer for the nest/self-explosion/hammer triad [yuan2023yolov5tl], YOLOv5x with Coordinate Attention + BiFPN (BC-YOLO) [bao2022bcyolo], YOLOv4 + parallel mixed attention for abnormal dampers (PMA-YOLO, 93.8% AP) [bao2021pmayolo], YOLOv8 + SENet_v2/LSK head modules [zhang2024slyolov8], and YOLOv9-E + Mish + GCNet [pradeep2025insulator]. The APET paper itself uses the nano tier (v5n 1.8M / v8n 3.2M / v11n 2.6M params) [tsai2025atli].

**Two-stage / cascade detectors.** Faster R-CNN [ren2017faster] underlies the accuracy-oriented lineage: improved-anchor insulator detection (AP 0.818) [zhao2019insulator], broken-insulator + nest fault location (~97.6% accuracy) [lei2019faultdetection], nest detection with ROI mining [li2020birdnesttower, li2020birdnestuav], FPN Faster R-CNN + HSV post-analysis (mAP 90.8/91.7) [zhao2021insulator], and multi-class defect detection with a condition index (Wire_10) [liang2020wire10]. Cascades that crop then re-detect are a recurring pattern: ILN→DDN on CPLID [tao2020insulator], eagle-eye SPP + YOLOv3-tiny in-RoI (~30 ms) [han2020eagle], Faster R-CNN + U-net for self-blast insulators (P 0.951 / R 0.955 at ~800 ms) [ling2019selfblast], and Exact R-CNN / CME-CNN for hi-res insulator defects (AP 88.7% at 1.1 FPS) [wen2021insulator]. SSD-based systems include the multi-level-perception ensemble [jiang2019ensemble], the two-stage-fine-tuned SSD [miao2019insulator], Felect's SSD+FPN [maduako2022felect], and CE-SSD's context-enhancement variant [su2024cessd].

**DETR / transformer / self-supervised.** PA-DETR adds a dilated encoder, TL-specific relative position encoding, and attribute heads to DETR for visually indistinguishable bolt defects (81.9 mAP, 41M params, 28 FPS) [zhang2023padetr]. HC-ViT is a hierarchical conv-ViT backbone pretrained with SC-MAE (masked reconstruction + contrastive) on unlabeled normal-component crops, reaching AP50 84.2 at 95.8M params [zhang2024hcvit]. These transformer approaches buy accuracy with 15–40× the parameters of a nano YOLO — the trade-off this project quantifies directly (YOLOv11n ties DINO-4scale on mAP at ~18× fewer params).

**Attention and lightweight modifications.** Cheap attention modules are the field's most common single lever: SE [yuan2023yolov5tl], SENet_v2 (+3.6 mAP) [zhang2024slyolov8], CA (+2.0 mAP, beats SE/CBAM) [bao2022bcyolo], GCNet [pradeep2025insulator], PMA [bao2021pmayolo], spatial region attention [zhang2022agmnet], and the shape-aware strip-convolution Damper Attention sized to the damper's ~1:3 aspect ratio (+8.9 mAP over attention-free at only +0.54M params; DA beat SE/GAM/CBAM/CA in the same slot) [zhang2024dsanet]. Lightweight backbone swaps (MobileNet + depthwise-separable convolutions) recur for speed [qiu2022lightweightyolov4, shuang2023rsin, vazquez2025wildfire].

**Non-CNN alternatives.** Pre-DL local-feature and morphology baselines [liao2015insulator, zhao2016multipatch] motivate the learned-feature transition; a hand-crafted RTV + improved-GrabCut pipeline still reports 95.76% accuracy for damper structural defects on a very small set [huang2023damper], and Siddiqui's system pairs a CNN detector with training-free handcrafted defect analyzers precisely because faulty-component images were scarce [siddiqui2020drone].

## 4. Transfer learning strategies

COCO [lin2014coco] and ImageNet pretraining is near-universal. The ATLI paper's 3-stage protocol (COCO scratch → feature extraction on target → low-LR unfreeze-all fine-tuning) yields >20% gains on the low-instance classes and matches or beats 300–600-epoch scratch training in less time; unfreezing all layers wins over freezing the first 10 [tsai2025atli]. The pattern has clear precedents: the two-stage fine-tuning SSD (generic insulator model → specific-type fine-tune) [miao2019insulator], ImageNet-TL Faster R-CNN [lei2019faultdetection, ling2019selfblast], staged fine-tuning of lightweight YOLOv4 [qiu2022lightweightyolov4], multi-stage migration learning [yuan2023yolov5tl, bao2021pmayolo], TL from the TF model zoo when scratch training on CPU took ~50 h [maduako2022felect], and early pretrained-CNN feature extraction (98.71% on 1,000 samples) [zhao2016multipatch]. The freeze-vs-unfreeze question traces to the foundational transferability analysis of Yosinski et al. (cited via [shakiba2022insulator]).

Two nuances matter for this project. First, **source-domain homogeneity**: the same group's wildfire work found a homogeneous source (FASDD, 95k curated images) massively outperforms COCO for a tiny target set (79.2 vs 64.8 mAP), while cascaded multi-stage TL added nothing over one TL stage [vazquez2025wildfire, vazquez2024wildfire]; cross-dataset insulator transfer also works well in-domain (IDID→CPLID mAP 96.92) [pradeep2025insulator]; and domain-specific self-supervised pretraining beat both supervised and SSL ImageNet-1K by +1.8–2.6 AP50-95 [zhang2024hcvit]. This contrasts with the project's own repeated finding that in-domain-style pretraining on the noisy `atli_source` pool and on community damper data produced genuine negative transfer — suggesting the deciding variable is source *quality and label consistency*, not domain proximity per se. Second, **TL is free at inference**: transfer learning changes accuracy and training time but not edge FPS/power [vazquez2025wildfire]. Note also that some "transfer learning" titles are loose — [pradeep2025insulator] describes no staged source-pretraining protocol, and [shakiba2022insulator] is whole-image classification with a frozen backbone, a recipe that contrasts with the detection-side finding (here and in [tsai2025atli]) that full unfreezing wins.

## 5. Small-object and damper/hardware-specific detection

The literature converges on the same diagnosis this project reached: **damper and small-hardware defects are a small-object problem, and resolution is the dominant lever.** The strongest evidence is STN PLAD: Stockbridge dampers average 2.89e3 px in 16.8-MP images, whole-image resizing yields damper AP 0.214, and a 4×4 tiling pipeline (MS-PAD) lifts it to 0.838 (+62.4 points) [vieiraesilva2021stnplad]. CPLID's cascade exists for the same reason — missing caps are ~40×40 px in 4608×3456 images and single-stage Faster R-CNN fails outright (F1 0.643 vs 0.933 cascaded) [tao2020insulator]. Sliding-window tiling at train and test time is likewise the fix in [yuan2023yolov5tl], hi-res preservation drives [wen2021insulator], and crop-then-detect appears in bolt work cited by [zhang2023padetr]. This is the published counterpart of the project's imgsz-1280 champion recipe and the DD-crop audit showing defects are mostly small-object.

Dampers are consistently among the hardest classes wherever they appear: damper AP 39.95 is one of TLDD's two worst classes across all models tested [su2024cessd]; shockproof-hammer damage is HC-ViT's worst class (AP75 37.1, only 184 annotations) [zhang2024hcvit]; damper "Damage" is the hardest class for every network on TLD (best 0.636 vs Normal 0.854) [zhang2024dsanet]; Felect's damper-fault class has the lowest recall (58–74%) despite the largest instance count [maduako2022felect]; and Siddiqui's damper-weight was his worst-detected component [siddiqui2020drone]. The survey confirms fitting-detection metrics run systematically below insulator metrics (e.g., pin defects mAP 54.3%, rust mAP 75.4%) [faisal2025powerline, zhang2022agmnet]. This places the project's Defective_Damper AP range (0.61 baseline → 0.81 champion under CV) in line with, and at the upper end of, the field.

Damper-specific architectural levers published so far: shape-aware strip-conv attention + shallow-feature (P2-level) fusion + single-level ASFF [zhang2024dsanet]; BiFPN weighted fusion + coordinate attention (damaged-damper AP +5.3 from architecture alone) [bao2022bcyolo]; parallel mixed attention + damper-specific K-means anchors [bao2021pmayolo]; double labeling (whole hammer + per-side hammer bodies) with an IoU counting rule [yuan2023yolov5tl]; geometric post-detection reasoning for slippage faults (AP 87.56 for damper detection) [liu2021slippage]; multitask detect + rust/abnormal classification [zhang2022agmnet]; and segmentation-based structural analysis [huang2023damper]. Detect-then-classify is the recurring escape from sibling normal/defective class confusion: PA-DETR shows direct defect detection loses ~3 points versus routing defect reasoning through attribute/classification heads [zhang2023padetr], and Hybrid-YOLO's YOLOv5+ResNet-18 split beats end-to-end multi-class YOLOv5/v6/v7 on classification quality [souza2023hybridyolo]; cascaded and detect-then-analyze designs [ling2019selfblast, zhao2021insulator, han2020eagle] follow the same logic. Small-object receptive-field alternatives to brute-force resolution include dilated context modules and smaller default boxes [su2024cessd], PA-DETR's dilated encoder (AP_S 44.9→52.1) [zhang2023padetr], LSK dynamic receptive fields [zhang2024slyolov8], multi-level perception ensembles [jiang2019ensemble], FPN [ren2017faster, maduako2022felect], and shape-clustered feature enhancement for pins (mAP ~0.724) [zhao2020pinmissing].

## 6. Class imbalance and augmentation

Every defect dataset in this collection is imbalanced toward normal components: ATLI 1,460 Normal vs 113 Defective dampers [tsai2025atli]; DVDI 3,610 vs 636 [bao2022bcyolo]; TLD Normal 6,860 vs defects 960–1,650 [zhang2024dsanet]; VIBD 6,270 normal vs 397–1,271 per defect [zhang2023padetr]; CPLID 3,560 intact vs 248 broken [shakiba2022insulator]; TLCD 2,143 self-explosion vs 83 shielded-ring-corrosion annotations [zhang2024hcvit].

**Loss-level remedies are strikingly rare in the damper literature.** Focal loss [lin2017focal] — the canonical dense-detection imbalance fix — appears in this collection only for foreground/background and class-frequency imbalance: YoloV4++ (+6.54 COCO mAP, evened per-class AP) [shuang2023rsin], Felect's SSD confidence loss (γ=2, α=0.25) [maduako2022felect], and nest-detection RPNs [li2020birdnestuav]. No damper paper applies focal or class-weighted loss to the normal-vs-defective imbalance — the gap the project's lit review identified, corroborated here.

**Data-level remedies dominate**, and the balanced-vs-big distinction is quantified: on CPLID, a class-balanced augmented set reaches 99.93% accuracy while an equal-size imbalanced augmented set gets 93.22% — balance, not volume, drives the gain [shakiba2022insulator]. Minority-targeted synthesis appears as U-Net background fusion (precision 62.58→94.19% across the ablation) [tao2020insulator], GraphCut copy-paste of defective insulators onto real backgrounds [qiu2022lightweightyolov4], Photoshop-simulated faults [maduako2022felect], 4–16× minority augmentation on CPLID [su2024cessd], and WGAN feature generation [gao2025zeroshot]. HC-ViT sidesteps the problem entirely by pretraining on the *abundant* side of the imbalance (unlabeled normal crops) [zhang2024hcvit]. SL-YOLOv8's discussion argues sample count alone saturates and recommends combining augmentation, resampling, and loss re-weighting [zhang2024slyolov8] — the resampling arm being exactly the project's 3× native oversampling.

**Which augmentations, specifically, is largely untested in the literature.** DVDI uses rotation (12°/180°) + flip + gamma [bao2022bcyolo] and rotation appears throughout [maduako2022felect, sadykova2020inyolo], but no paper in this collection ablates rotation for detection — the project's finding that rotation *hurts* detection (deg10: DD −0.10; deg45: box inflation) while scale-down aug helps has no published counterpart. Laplacian sharpening preprocessing (+3.45 mAP) [qiu2022lightweightyolov4] is a cheap untried lever. Evaluation rigor is also thin: multi-run reporting exists only in DSA-Net (3-run averages) [zhang2024dsanet], SL-YOLOv8 (10 Monte Carlo re-splits with t-tests) [zhang2024slyolov8], STN PLAD (5-split Monte Carlo CV) [vieiraesilva2021stnplad], the wildfire thesis (stratified 5-fold CV, reporting AP std) [vazquez2025wildfire], and [ling2019selfblast] (3/4-fold CV); everything else is a single split.

## 7. Real-time and edge deployment

The survey finds only 33% of papers consider real-time suitability, with ~30 FPS on low-powered edge devices as the common target [faisal2025powerline]. On-drone/embedded precedents: modified YOLOv3 on a Jetson TX2 in live video [siddiqui2020drone]; DSA-Net at 7.2 ms (TITAN XP) and 30-FPS RTSP field operation on an RTX3060 laptop, robust down to 720P [zhang2024dsanet]; lightweight YOLOv4 at 53 FPS / 12.75M params [qiu2022lightweightyolov4]; YoloV4++ at 53.8 FPS with a 5× parameter cut [shuang2023rsin]; CE-SSD at 40 FPS (though its fusion halves SSD's native speed) [su2024cessd]; edge-cloud MobileNet-SSD fusion for self-explosion monitoring (cited in [faisal2025powerline]). The closest methodological template is the same group's wildfire work: MobileNetV3-Small backbone + GhostConv neck + halved channels (0.82M params) exported to OpenVINO reaches 31.9 FPS on a CPU-only Raspberry Pi 5 (423% over base PyTorch YOLOv5n) with 11.4% lower power, at a 79.2→71.2 mAP cost; YOLOv5n runs ~2× faster than YOLO11n on CPU [vazquez2025wildfire].

Against this backdrop, the accuracy-per-parameter argument is stark: PA-DETR needs 41M params / 28 FPS [zhang2023padetr] and HC-ViT 95.8M with no reported FPS [zhang2024hcvit], while several "lightweight" papers report no measured FPS at all [bao2022bcyolo, maduako2022felect, pradeep2025insulator, zhang2024slyolov8] or defer quantization/pruning to future work [pradeep2025insulator, bao2022bcyolo]. The APET paper itself leaves real-time/edge evaluation to future work [tsai2025atli] — the gap the project's TensorRT-FP16 Jetson campaign (~18 fps projected at 768) fills. Blur robustness is nearly untested in the literature (deblurring appears only via a cited DeblurGANv2 pipeline in [zhang2024slyolov8]'s references), whereas the project measured severe blur fragility (7px motion blur: −0.246 mAP).

## 8. Gap analysis — what the literature does not solve

1. **Rare-class native oversampling vs external data.** The literature's answer to defect scarcity is synthesis [tao2020insulator, qiu2022lightweightyolov4, gao2025zeroshot], architecture [zhang2024dsanet, bao2022bcyolo], or in-domain pretraining [zhang2024hcvit]. No paper tests harvesting community/web datasets for a rare class and none reports the negative result this project established: raw external damper data never improved Defective_Damper AP at any mixing dose (label/domain mismatch causes a recall drop), while simple 3× native oversampling + hi-res + scale aug did — with the in-domain control (~0.91 DD AP) proving the external labels are learnable but non-transferable. The closest published signal is the FASDD-vs-COCO source comparison [vazquez2025wildfire], which shows source choice matters but used a large *curated* homogeneous source; the project's result refines this to: source consistency with the target's label style, not mere domain proximity, is what transfers.
2. **Decontamination rigor.** CPLID's images (and DVDI's) propagate silently through derived datasets [shuang2023rsin, qiu2022lightweightyolov4, su2024cessd, bao2022bcyolo], and CPLID benchmarks are saturated partly for this reason [vieiraesilva2021stnplad, pradeep2025insulator]. No reviewed paper performs a pHash/filename leakage audit or reports before/after-decontamination numbers; the project's CPLID purge (test-set decontamination with the champion margin surviving: 0.784 vs 0.736 clean) has no published precedent.
3. **CV-based per-class evaluation of rare classes.** With ≤39 test instances of a rare class, single-split per-class AP swings by ±0.14 (the project measured 0.61–0.88 across folds). Only 5 of the 18 full reads use any repeated-split protocol [zhang2024dsanet, zhang2024slyolov8, vieiraesilva2021stnplad, vazquez2025wildfire, ling2019selfblast], and none reports per-rare-class CV means ± std as the headline metric. The APET paper's own single 70/15/15 split with a tiny DD test pool [tsai2025atli] is exactly the weakness the project's multi-seed / proper-CV protocol repairs.
4. **Nano-model vs DETR/transformer trade-off, measured on one benchmark.** The literature offers heavyweight transformers with knowledge reasoning [zhang2023padetr, zhang2024hcvit] and nano YOLOs [tsai2025atli, vazquez2025wildfire] in disjoint papers on disjoint datasets. The project's controlled comparison — YOLOv11n (2.6M) tying DINO-4scale (47M) on CV mAP (0.785) and beating it on Defective_Damper AP (0.812 vs 0.789) — is a datapoint the literature lacks, and it strengthens the edge case beyond what any single published comparison shows.
5. **Smaller open gaps.** Focal/class-weighted loss for normal-vs-defective imbalance remains untried field-wide (Section 6); augmentation ablations (rotation harmful, scale-aug sweet spot, close_mosaic) are absent; OBB task-mode comparisons for TL defects do not appear in this collection; and blur robustness of deployed models is unmeasured.
